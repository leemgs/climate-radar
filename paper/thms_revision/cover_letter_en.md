Dear Dr. Trajkovic,

We sincerely thank you, the Associate Editor, and the three reviewers for the careful evaluation of manuscript THMS-26-02-0115. As invited in your decision letter of 12 November 2026, we have considered every comment and now submit a substantially revised manuscript as a new submission.

The Associate Editor identified three main concerns: the novelty of the contribution, methodological transparency, and the alignment of the claims with the evidence. We agree with these concerns. In particular, we accepted Reviewer 3's observation about the Joint Cognitive Systems (JCS) literature and have fundamentally repositioned the paper. The revised manuscript no longer presents action-centered evaluation as a new concept. Instead, it presents a concrete application of established human factors principles to AI-based disaster decision support, together with an empirical evaluation.

The main changes are as follows.

1. **Positioning and contributions.** ACHDF is now explicitly framed as an instantiation, for disaster response, of the JCS perspective (Hollnagel & Woods, 2005; Woods & Hollnagel, 2006) and of adaptive function allocation (Parasuraman et al., 2000; Kaber & Endsley, 2004; Scerbo, 2007). A new Related Work subsection (II-A) explains how ACHDF relates to this body of work, and all three contributions have been rewritten.

2. **Definition of constructs.** A new Table I gives the role and operational definition of uncertainty, criticality, guardrails, bounded autonomy, escalation, trust, workload, and action execution, distinguishing design inputs, system modes, design considerations, and outcomes. Throughout the manuscript, we now state consistently that ACHDF adapts only the *level and form of AI support*, and that decision authority always remains with the human operator.

3. **Methodological transparency.** New subsections and figures describe the interfaces of both conditions, the task and scenarios (including a worked example), the training procedure, the participants and ethics approval, the power justification, and all instruments (NASA-TLX, a three-item trust measure with consistent terminology, and the SUS). The primary outcome, the Action Execution Rate (AER), is now decomposed into a correct-action rate and an on-time rate, so that failures due to wrong actions can be distinguished from failures due to delay.

4. **Statistics.** A new Statistical Analysis subsection specifies the tests, assumption checks, 95% confidence intervals, effect-size measures (d_z / rank-biserial), multiplicity correction, and order-effect analysis. All outcomes are now reported together, with their statistics, in a single table (Table V).

5. **Consistency and redundancy.** Numerical inconsistencies between the text and the tables have been corrected. Overlapping Results subsections (former V-A/V-B) were merged, redundant tables were consolidated into Tables V and VI, and the single Cohen's d whose comparison was unclear has been removed.

6. **Qualified claims.** Claims regarding mechanism, trust calibration, automation bias, the benefit of the action format, and cross-domain generalizability have been removed or reframed as hypotheses. Correspondingly, the Limitations section now addresses four points: the bundled nature of the intervention, the absence of a direct test of calibration and automation bias, domain and population generalizability, and the ecological validity of deferral.

7. **Structure and language.** The Results section now reports only descriptive and inferential findings. The former "mechanism analysis" has been moved to the Discussion and recast as a framework-based interpretation (Section VI-B). The manuscript has been edited for clarity and flow, inappropriate transitions ("in fact", "on the other hand") have been removed, and eleven references from the human factors literature have been added.

A detailed point-by-point response to the reviewers and a marked-up comparison of the original and revised manuscripts (latexdiff) are enclosed. We believe the revision has made the contribution and its evidential basis considerably clearer, and we would be grateful for your further consideration.

Sincerely,
